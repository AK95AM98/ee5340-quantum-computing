    Chapters

    This textbook is organized into 16 chapters covering 200 concepts.

    Chapter Overview

    1. Mathematical Foundations - Core mathematical tools including complex numbers, linear algebra, matrix operations, eigenvalue theory, and probability.
    2. Physical Limits of Computing - Thermodynamic constraints on computation from Landauer's principle through ultimate speed and information limits.
    3. Information Theory Foundations - Shannon entropy, mutual information, coding theorems, channel capacity, and algorithmic information theory.
    4. Classical Computing Fundamentals - Boolean algebra, CMOS physics, power dissipation, the Boltzmann tyranny, and computational complexity.
    5. Beyond-CMOS Devices - Emerging device technologies including spintronics, memristors, tunnel FETs, and negative capacitance FETs.
    6. Reversible Computing - Logical and physical reversibility, Bennett's proof, Toffoli and Fredkin gates, and adiabatic computing.
    7. Quantum Mechanics Foundations - Postulates of quantum mechanics, Dirac notation, Hilbert space, measurement, and the Schrodinger equation.
    8. Qubits and Quantum States - Qubit representation, Bloch sphere, entanglement, Bell states, and Schmidt decomposition.
    9. Quantum Gates and Circuits - Pauli gates, Hadamard, rotation operators, CNOT, universal gate sets, and the quantum circuit model.
    10. Quantum Algorithms - Deutsch-Jozsa, QFT, phase estimation, Shor's algorithm, Grover's search, and quantum walks.
    11. Quantum Error Correction - Noise channels, Kraus operators, stabilizer formalism, surface codes, and fault tolerance.
    12. Quantum Hardware Platforms - Superconducting qubits, trapped ions, photonic qubits, and topological qubits.
    13. Stochastic Computing - Stochastic number encoding, logic gates, variance analysis, and applications.
    14. Quantum-Classical Hybrid Computing - VQE, QAOA, parameterized circuits, barren plateaus, and NISQ-era constraints.
    15. Energy-Efficient Computing Paradigms - Neuromorphic, approximate, in-memory, and thermodynamic computing.
    16. Future of Computing - Quantum supremacy, quantum advantage, benchmarking, and the post-Moore landscape.

    How to Use This Textbook

    Chapters are ordered to respect concept dependencies — each chapter builds on material from previous chapters. Students should work through chapters sequentially, as later topics assume mastery of earlier material. The mathematical foundations in Chapter 1 are referenced throughout the entire textbook.

    Note: Each chapter includes a list of concepts covered. Make sure to complete prerequisites before moving to advanced chapters.
